## The short version

1. **One essay a week runs the whole machine.** Each weekly Substack letter goes out as an X Article on the same day with the same title (7 of his top 10 X Articles match a letter, SEEN). Short lines from it become X posts, 12 of his 15 Threads posts are copied word for word from X the same day, and the same quote images go to Instagram and LinkedIn (same day or next). He described this system in 2022: "1 piece of content per week… ~2 hours a day" (SEEN, self-reported).
2. **Long form wins on X now.** X Articles are 15 of his 23 top X posts, with a median of 2.0M views against 384K for his other posts (5.2x, SEEN). His pinned Article "How to fix your entire life in 1 day" (2026-01-12) has **250.6M views and 964K bookmarks** (SEEN).
3. **He grew slowly, then jumped when he went multi-platform.** By his own count: 4K followers (Feb 2020), 25K (Dec 2020), 69K (Nov 2021). In Sept 2023 he wrote "Year 1: 10K. Year 2: 50K. Year 3: 100K. (Doubled down and diversified platforms.) Year 4: 2.8M" (SEEN, self-reported). His YouTube breakouts (Oct and Dec 2022) and his most-liked Instagram posts (Dec 2022) fall in that window (SEEN).
4. **The look is monochrome and faceless, except on YouTube.** 0 of his 18 top Instagram posts show his face, and 18 of 18 top LinkedIn posts are white text on black. 16 of 16 top YouTube thumbnails show his face (SEEN).
5. **The money comes from his own products, not sponsors.** He sells 5 courses at $99–$299, a $499 bundle and $39–$49 upsells, plus his app Eden. 0 of 10 YouTube videos checked carry a sponsor (SEEN).

**First play to run this week:** write one 1,500-word essay and publish it natively as an X Article, then cut it into 5 short posts and 3 text-on-black images (plays 1–3).

## How the posts are made

### X
- **Pattern:** 15 of 23 top posts are **X Articles** (0 of 46 bottom). Among regular posts, hook length and wording show no clear difference between top and bottom (a median of 24 vs 25 words in the first line): on X, the format is the lever (SEEN).

Articles run 2,700–5,600 words and open with a confession or a callout: "Tell me if any of these sound like you:" (SEEN).

## Ten plays for you (stage: starting out)

### 1. Write one essay a week and cut it into everything
- **Evidence:** his 2022 claim (1 piece of content a week); 2026 data: letter → X Article same day (7 of top 10), X → Threads (12 of 15), IG ↔ LinkedIn (same images). SEEN.
- **Effort:** a week to set up, then ~2 hours a day · **First result:** 2 weeks · **Metric:** posts made from each essay (aim for 10+).

```prompt
You are my content editor. My niche is [YOUR NICHE] and my audience is [WHO]. 
Step 1: Help me pick this week's essay topic: give me 10 titles using these formulas: "How to become so [trait] it feels illegal", "You need to be [extreme trait] if you want to [goal]", "How to [outcome] (in [time frame])". 
Step 2: After I choose, outline a 1,500-word essay that opens with a personal confession ("These past few weeks, I've felt...") or a callout ("Tell me if any of these sound like you:"). 
Step 3: Once I paste my draft, cut it into: 5 standalone X posts (first line under 10 words, no hashtags), 3 short quote cards (a 4-6 word first line, then 3-5 short sentences), and 1 LinkedIn post. Keep my voice blunt and direct.
```

### 2. Publish the essay natively as an X Article
- **Evidence:** Articles are 15 of his 23 top X posts, with a median of 2.0M views against 384K for other posts; his pinned Article has 250.6M views. SEEN. Your numbers will be much smaller; the point is that X gives native long form a lift (INFERRED).
- **Effort:** a day · **First result:** 1 week · **Metric:** Article views ÷ your median post views.

```prompt
Turn my essay below into an X Article. Keep the title from the essay. Write a 1-2 sentence opening that works as a standalone preview (it shows in the feed). Break it into short sections with bold subheads, keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences, and end with one line that points to my newsletter: [LINK]. Essay: [PASTE]
```

## Method notes

- **Calls:** 118 treg calls. AnyAPI for X, Instagram and YouTube; HarvestAPI for LinkedIn; ScrapeCreators for Threads; Crawl4AI for Substack; treg.web.search and web.extract (free) for his pages. Wayback Machine (free) for old YouTube snapshots.
- **Skipped:** no platforms. Threads has only 15 posts, so no pattern is called there.
- **Limits:** the X timeline reaches back only to Jan 27, 2026 (100 posts). Older X years come from 20 "Top" posts per year, which favors hits. Instagram's 3 pinned posts from 2022–23 are excluded from ranking. YouTube dates are approximate except for the top 15 videos. Instagram Reels are ranked by likes + comments, not views. Topic labels are keyword-based, so 36–53% of posts on some platforms land in "other". Wayback: X snapshots could not be read (the pages need JavaScript), and most other requests were rate-limited. Social Blade had no snapshots. YouTube Shorts thumbnails were downloaded but not reviewed.
- **Not used:** follower lists, contact details and his location (the rules forbid them).
- **Claims:** income and follower numbers in his own posts are self-reported. The $250K prize is a third-party claim.
